## Thread A — The money (the thread the audit only scratched)

### Q1. What exactly was the Digital News Innovation Challenge money? ★ highest yield
- **Provenance:** Contradiction-pass unresolved lead (Validator B). CB Insights lists DNIC as an investor; a Carleton journal records $100,000 in funding plus Facebook ad credits tied to Ground's participation, from Kaur's own account.
- **Already known:** DNIC was a Canadian programme partnered with the Facebook Journalism Project. It was not equity.
- **The dig needs:** The Carleton article in full; DNIC / Facebook Journalism Project programme records; any Ground disclosure of the grant anywhere (site, press, interviews); the programme's terms (what did recipients owe Meta/Facebook?).
- **What could move:** GN-09's "that limb held" note — either the big-tech limb genuinely closes, or Ground's "no ties to big tech" FAQ line gains a documented exception its readers were never told about.

### Q2. What has Ground News actually raised, in total, and from whom?
- **Provenance:** GN-09 — four databases give $20K / $170K / $340K / $1.01M and conflate the uCiC/Snapwise predecessor era with Ground News.
- **Already known:** Snapwise incorporated 2014; named investors across databases (Techstars, Right Side Capital, Something Good Ventures, 37 Angels, Blue Lagoon, Velocity, NaHCO3); PitchBook counts 10 investors and an undisclosed Later Stage VC on 15 May 2025.
- **The dig needs:** A raise-by-raise reconstruction separating legal eras (uCiC 2014–16 vs Ground News 2017– ); each investor's own portfolio page as primary confirmation; the Velocity Fund II amount, if any portfolio or university source states it.
- **What could move:** GN-09 from "contradicted with a messy record" to a clean, dated money ledger — and the "(total raised)" figure becomes Confirmed or permanently Unverifiable with the reason shown.

### Q3. Is Ground actually subscriber-supported — or sponsor-supported?
- **Provenance:** Mission claim ("Subscription not Ads… supported by our subscribers") vs Test 3 — #1 most-sponsored brand on YouTube, H1 2025 (1,863 integrations, 664M views, Gospel Stats sample).
- **Already known:** No revenue figures are public. App revenue is estimable (Sensor Tower ranges); sponsorship *volume* is documented, spend is not.
- **The dig needs:** Sponsorship-rate estimation from creator-economy rate cards for channels of the sizes Ground sponsors; app/subscription revenue estimates; any statement by Ground or its growth staff about channel mix (podcasts with its growth director exist).
- **What could move:** A core Mission principle becomes testable. If estimated sponsorship spend dwarfs plausible subscription revenue, "Subscription not Ads" is a values statement about a business that runs on ads of another kind. Labelled as estimation throughout — ranges, not false precision.

### Q4. What does Ground pay its raters — and what does the unpaid one think now?
- **Provenance:** Test 6 — CJR on record: Ad Fontes is paid; MBFC's founder said no agreement; AllSides' COO said its ratings are used without formal permission or compensation. Ground declined specifics.
- **The dig needs:** ◆ Follow-ups to the three raters (statements, not ambushes); Ad Fontes' published licensing terms for commercial users; whether AllSides' position has changed since CJR (Sep 2025).
- **What could move:** The word "independent" in GN-03/GN-04 — a paid rater, an unpaid unconsenting rater, and an uncontracted rater are three different relationships currently averaged into one adjective.

### Q5. What is the Straight Arrow News partnership, in writing?
- **Provenance:** GN-13 (added by Validator B) — Ricketts-owned Straight Arrow "operates in partnership with Ground News for some of its news output"; Snapwise director Manuel Mattke is its advisory-board member and president/COO of Ricketts' Opportunity Education.
- **Already known:** The partnership's existence, from Straight Arrow / secondary reporting. No terms public.
- **The dig needs:** Straight Arrow's own pages on the partnership; what "some of its news output" concretely means (data feed? ratings licence? content?); Mattke's roles dated against the partnership's start.
- **What could move:** GN-13 stays Unverifiable by design as a *motive* claim — but a documented content partnership with an ideologically positioned outlet is a structural fact that would sit beside it. Careful: no funding inference; the audit's discipline on this point holds.

## Thread C — Replicate the method

### Q9. Can anyone rebuild Ground's bias composite from the three raters' scores?
- **Provenance:** GN-03 — averaging verified to occur; the mapping/formula is unpublished, so the composite is irreproducible as filed.
- **The dig needs:** 20 outlets × the three raters' current published ratings × Ground's displayed overall rating; attempt fits (simple average after rescaling, median, relative-position methods) and report what matches and what doesn't.
- **What could move:** Either the method is reverse-engineered (and the audit can say how it works), or irreproducibility is demonstrated outlet-by-outlet instead of asserted. Both are findings.

### Q10. How wrong is a US spectrum outside the US?
- **Provenance:** GN-13 structural note — a US left–right spectrum applied to a corpus sold as global (50,000 sources "from around the world").
- **The dig needs:** A sample of Canadian, UK, and Indian outlets; compare Ground's labels against each country's own political geography (e.g. a Canadian centre-right paper filed under US "Right," a UK public broadcaster filed "Lean Left").
- **What could move:** Turns a generic criticism into a measured misclassification sample — or clears it.

### Q11. How accurate is the hand-coded ownership dataset?
- **Provenance:** GN-12 — 2,276 outlets, self-reported, never independently audited.
- **The dig needs:** A random 30-outlet sample checked against corporate/registry and outlet-about-page records.
- **What could move:** The only one of Ground's datasets that Ground built itself gets an error rate. Clean or dirty, that's a verdict with a denominator the methodology *can* pay for.
